What to Do After a House Fire: Your Contents Claim, Step by Step

A house fire turns your life upside down in an afternoon. I spent years as a property claims adjuster, and I've walked through a lot of burned homes with families who didn't know where to start. This is the order I'd do things in if it were my house.

1. Make sure everyone is safe, then call your insurance company

Report the claim as soon as you can. Most policies say you have to give "prompt notice," and calling early gets an adjuster assigned faster. Write down your claim number and your adjuster's name and phone number the first time you hear them. You'll use them constantly.

2. Ask about temporary housing right away

If you can't live in the house, most homeowners policies include Additional Living Expense (sometimes called Loss of Use or Coverage D). It can pay for a hotel or rental, the extra cost of eating out, and extra mileage while your home is being repaired. Keep every receipt. Ask your adjuster what the limit is and how they want receipts sent.

3. Protect the property from more damage

Your policy expects you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage, like boarding up windows or tarping the roof. Most insurers will pay for this, and many have companies they can send out. Save the invoice.

4. Don't throw anything away yet

This is the one people regret most. Your adjuster may want to see damaged items before they're tossed. If something has to go for health reasons, like food or soaked carpet, photograph it first, including any labels or brand names.

5. Take photos of everything, room by room

Before cleanup starts, walk every room and take wide photos from each corner, then close-ups. Open closets, drawers and cabinets and photograph what's inside. Smoke reaches places you wouldn't expect. Photos of brand tags and model labels are worth their weight in gold later.

6. Start your contents list early

The contents list (some insurers call it a personal property inventory or proof of loss list) is usually the slowest part of a fire claim. It's a line-by-line list of everything that was damaged or destroyed, with:

  • What the item is, plus the brand and model if you know it
  • How many you had
  • About how old it was
  • What it costs to replace it new today

Go room by room. Use old photos on your phone, social media posts, online order history and bank statements to jog your memory. The most commonly forgotten areas are closets, the garage, the attic, the pantry and bathroom cabinets.

7. Be specific

An adjuster can only pay for what's on the list, and only as well as it's described. "TV" usually gets priced as a basic TV. "Samsung 65 inch QLED, about 3 years old" gets priced as what you actually had. The same goes for furniture, clothing and appliances.

8. Understand how you'll be paid

Many policies pay the actual cash value first, which is the replacement cost minus depreciation for age and wear. If your policy has replacement cost coverage, you can usually collect the depreciation that was held back after you replace the item. I explain this in How depreciation works on a contents claim.

The short version

Report it fast, keep every receipt, don't toss anything before it's photographed, and build a detailed list. If you do those four things, your claim will move faster and pay closer to what you actually lost.
